About

I arrived at Hogwarts in 1991 knowing nothing about the wizarding world and everything about libraries. That combination turned out to be the whole job. Muggle-born witches and wizards see the seams in magical institutions because we were not raised to look past them.

My practice sits where research meets statute. I read the primary sources, I count who is excluded, and then I write the amendment. It is slower than a duel and it lasts longer.

Before leading Magical Law Enforcement I spent six years in the Department for the Regulation and Control of Magical Creatures, where I wrote the framework that ended non-consensual binding of house-elves. I still teach one seminar a year, because a reform nobody understands is a reform that gets repealed.

  • Based in London, United Kingdom
  • Languages English, Mermish (reading), Gobbledegook (conversational)

03

Selected projects

Four pieces of work I would want to be judged on.

  • The Elf Labour Act

    Legislation · 2016

    Three years of household surveys turned into eleven clauses. Wage floors, the right to refuse an order, and a complaints body that does not report to any employing family.

    • Drafting
    • Field survey
    • Enforcement design

  • Open Detention Register

    Transparency · 2021

    Every Azkaban detention, its legal basis, and its review date, published quarterly. Building it required digitising 60 years of parchment and admitting what the gaps meant.

    • Records
    • Public data
    • Accountability

  • Centaur Border Accords

    Negotiation · 2018

    A treaty written in two legal traditions at once, recognising herd sovereignty over the Forbidden Forest while keeping student access through a narrow, agreed corridor.

    • Treaty
    • Mediation
    • Comparative law

  • Wizengamot Conviction Review

    Research · 2004–2013

    A reproducible method for auditing wartime convictions: source the original testimony, test it against the charge, and publish the reasoning whether or not it flatters the court.

    • Methodology
    • Archives
    • Appeals
